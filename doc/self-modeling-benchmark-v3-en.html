01

Discover limits, explain constraints, adapt the strategy

An embodied capability discovery and adaptive planning benchmark. Give an agent an unfamiliar body and world, a documented action interface, and a natural-language goal. Evaluate discovery of unknown capability limits, composition of environmental resources, and evidence-driven adaptation of long-horizon strategies.

Unknown capability → budgeted interaction → calibrated self/world belief → resource composition → feasible strategy → execution / recovery

Capability discovery

Use informative interactions to estimate reach, load, grasping, mobility, and contact limits. Command syntax is public; Explicit / Partial / Latent controls physical disclosure. Hidden API rules are not the source of difficulty.

Resourceful planning

When direct action is insufficient, compose boxes, supports, tools, carts, doors, or partners. Resources change the feasible solution set; geometry, forces, and control must support each route.

Strategy adaptation

Hold world and goal fixed while changing the body or validated control capability. Test identification of invalid steps, strategy reconstruction, and appropriate invariance when a change is unnecessary.

Research question: can an embodied agent discover physical and embodiment-specific constraints in an unfamiliar world, form calibrated capability beliefs, and compose available resources into a feasible long-horizon strategy?

Positioning: EmbodiedSWE already addresses coding-agent robot solving, solution transfer, trajectory generation, and agent improvement. Capability discovery and adaptive planning become our primary framing; Terminal-Bench remains a historical analogy. The proposed contribution combines controlled disclosure, capability interventions, and diagnostic evaluation. More tasks, Genesis, or G1 alone do not establish novelty. Official EmbodiedSWE repository.

Retain Benchmark v2's four axes, five metrics, and paired experiments, plus v1's composite tasks. The V3 method proposal [external workspace] remains separate. Explicit capability memory is a hypothesis, not an entry requirement. Tasks, thresholds, and interfaces below await implementation and physical validation.

02

A joint belief over self and world

Instance I = (T, E, C, O, W) hₜ = (o₁:ₜ, a₁:ₜ₋₁, r₁:ₜ₋₁) bₜ(C, W) = P(C, W | hₜ, disclosure(O)) Ĉₜ, Ŵₜ = summaries of bₜ; π(aₜ | T, hₜ, Ĉₜ, Ŵₜ) F(a; s) = f(C_robot, P_object, G_geometry, D_dynamics; s)

T is the goal; E the body/team and controller; C the latent, context-dependent capability profile; O the disclosure policy; W the world physical parameters. E constrains C. The agent receives normal observations o and only the capability information permitted by O, rather than the full private C and W.

bₜ is a belief distribution, while Ĉₜ and Ŵₜ are estimates or summaries. Feedback r means observable physical execution evidence, not hidden reward, feasibility labels, or verifier output. Feasibility depends jointly on self, object properties, geometry, and dynamics. A scalar reach or payload threshold is meaningful only under a specified calibration context.

  1. Observe and form hypotheses.Use body appearance, proprioception, prior knowledge, and disclosed specifications.
  2. Attempt or probe.Choose an informative, budgeted interaction when uncertainty matters.
  3. Update self and world models.Retain competing explanations for ambiguous feedback.
  4. Identify the gap and construct a workaround.Reposition, extend reach, create support, bridge a gap, redistribute load, or recruit a partner.
  5. Execute and revise.Check observable outcomes and update the plan within the episode.

Effective capability is the set of goals attainable under the public validity constraints and budget, using permitted resources and partners. Distinguish physical feasibility from the deployed policy's probability of success; a failed controller rollout alone does not prove an impossible action.

Goal: retrieve the red box from a 1.3 m shelf. The target, platform, pole, receiving surface, and capable partner are present in every variant. Only the focal robot's body/controller changes. The values below are evaluator-side illustrations, withheld from the agent in Latent mode.

B / REACH 1.0 m + STEPPING

Infer reach gap → move platform → step → grasp

Infer own limits → allocate partner → receive target

Independently validate the partner's reach, load capacity, transfer, and coordination. Partner access is identical in A–D; its work and communication count toward cost.

These are candidate routes, not four unique gold plans. A shared partner or tool can yield a feasible strategy for several bodies. Score required switching only when the selected route becomes infeasible or feasible sets are disjoint. Report efficiency-preferred switching under a declared cost objective separately, and credit appropriate invariance when the same route remains suitable.

Separate clean interventions from this four-body illustration

A–D combines several body differences for explanation. The diagnostic pilot varies one validated capability axis at a time: reach geometry, force/torque envelope, jaw range, or mobility/control. Hold goal, world parameters, resources, partner, budget, O, and compatible interface fixed. Revalidate collisions, viewpoints, and controller competence; changing metadata alone is not a physical intervention.

Invariant, unsolvable, and uncertain controls

Include irrelevant or cosmetic body changes, shared feasible strategies, and cases with no permitted solution. Credit evidence-based uncertainty and abstention. A changed strategy is useful only when it matches validated feasibility or cost differences.

G1, a high cabinet, and movable boxes: discovering and executing a solution

In the support route above, the agent identifies a potentially movable, load-bearing box, then attempts transport, placement, stepping, and retrieval. A candidate program is inspect → move(crate, cabinet) → place → step_onto → reach → grasp(target). These are proposed benchmark skill semantics, not a complete API already supplied by a controller.

After slipping or a failed step, visible contact, pose, and displacement evidence can motivate a revised stability estimate, placement, or permitted route. A wheeled dual-arm body may need a tool or partner; a body without grasping may need a transfer mechanism or a justified infeasibility report. Body names do not establish feasibility: each step and transition requires validation.

04

Four task categories + one Composite Challenge Split

Task × Constraint × Embodiment × Capability Knowledge Access / Manipulation / Environmental Augmentation / Collaboration / Composite

Task defines the demand or augmentation mechanism; Constraint identifies the physical limitation; Embodiment specifies the body/team; Capability Knowledge controls disclosure. These are separate design axes, not a claim of statistical independence or universal combinability. Four main task categories are reporting groups; Composite is the fifth task-axis value, reported as a separate challenge split.

01 / ACCESS

Mobility & Access

The goal is known, but the robot's mobility and the environment do not match. Examples include a passage narrower than the robot, a blocked door, push-versus-pull access, steps, gaps, and a longer feasible detour.

Test: mobility constraint → alternative access strategy. Validate route width, turning clearance, step/terrain control, and whether objects can actually be moved.

02 / MANIPULATION

Manipulation

Grasping, lifting, holding, and reorienting are manipulation demands. Reach, payload, gripper, dexterity, and geometry are cross-cutting constraints. Illustrations include a target at 1.4 m versus a 1.0 m reach envelope, an 8 kg load versus a validated 3 kg lifting limit, and an object wider than the gripper.

Test: predict which direct action is infeasible before attempting it. These numbers describe controlled examples; object mass stays private. A single-arm lid-holding task is infeasible only when sequential and support-based alternatives are excluded by validated conditions.

03 / ENVIRONMENT

Environmental Augmentation

Resources alter effective capability. Tool use is a subclass: the useful resource may be an ordinary box, board, table, or cart.

  • Extend: a pole extends interaction reach.
  • Support: a stable platform changes effective height.
  • Bridge: a board enlarges accessible workspace.
  • Mechanical advantage / load redistribution: a lever, pulley, or cart changes force or transport requirements.

Moving a table can also create an intermediate reachable surface. Each benefit depends on verified contact and control conditions.

04 / COLLABORATION

Collaborative Capability Augmentation

A robot recognizes that a partner can compensate for its limitation. Examples include a mobile robot transporting an object into a strong fixed arm's workspace, coordinated lifting, and a narrow-access robot handing an object to a high-reaching robot.

A weak arm cannot simply carry an 8 kg object: validate a cart/push/drag transport mechanism. Likewise, m > P_A, m > P_B, m < P_A + P_B is only a screening condition for joint lifting; load sharing, grasp geometry, coordination, and actuator limits still need validation.

Annotation rule: Access and Manipulation describe task demands; Environment and Collaboration describe augmentation mechanisms. They can overlap. Assign a predeclared primary category and secondary deficit/mechanism tags, report overlap, and count each family once in the allocation. Do not reveal these solution-relevant labels to the agent.

Composite Challenge Split · combine several capability gaps

Goal: bring the red box from the attic to the kitchen.

A proposed scene has blocked stairs, a board in a storage room, a heavy cabinet blocking that room, and another robot nearby. One candidate chain is:

  1. Diagnose the access problem.Identify why the current route is unavailable.
  2. Discover a potential ramp resource.The board may support an alternate route, but is inaccessible.
  3. Recruit a capable partner.Coordinate clearing the cabinet, subject to validated force and grasp limits.
  4. Retrieve and position the board.Check length, slope, anchoring, load, and whether the embodiment can traverse it.
  5. Retrieve and deliver the target.Complete the final state through this or another permitted solution.

This is a candidate scenario, not a working physical solution. The board must create a valid route; a humanoid label does not imply ramp or stair competence. A composite task combines multiple primary-category demands and nested prerequisites rather than merely concatenating independent puzzles.

The earlier awareness → environment → collaboration progression remains an assessment lens, not a competing taxonomy. Generic APIs and hidden-verifier boundaries stay unchanged. Team composition, communication, and partner effort must be controlled and logged.

05

Constraints cross every task family

A physical limit can block access, manipulation, environmental augmentation, or collaboration. Keep constraint tags separate from the primary task category; multiple tags can apply to one instance.

  • KinematicReach envelope, joint limits, and motion feasibility.
  • DynamicActuator force/torque, load, acceleration, and lifting limits.
  • GeometricClearance, object fit, collision, and grasp geometry.
  • Contact / frictionTraction, sliding resistance, contact pose, and jamming.
  • StabilitySupport, tipping, balance, and load distribution.
  • MorphologicalBody width/height, number of arms, DoF, and locomotion structure.
  • Multi-agent dependencyComplementary capabilities, synchronized actions, and role prerequisites.

Why did the box not move?

Insufficient robot force, high object mass, high friction, geometric jamming, and poor contact are competing hypotheses. One failed push generally does not identify a unique cause. The agent should update both self and world beliefs, state uncertainty, and select a discriminating interaction when useful.

Attribution diagnostic: construct matched force-only, mass-only, friction-only, clearance-only, and contact-only interventions. Keep all other relevant variables fixed, validate the causal change, and score the agent's predictions against private evaluator labels. Include multi-cause and observationally ambiguous cases; a self–world interaction may be the correct explanation.

Constraint and mechanism tags, interventions, and causal labels belong to evaluator metadata. Agents receive ordinary physical feedback, not an API that names the hidden cause. Explanations earn credit only where supported by predictions, probes, and outcomes.

06

Embodiment is a separate axis

Fixed Arm

Manipulation within a fixed workspace; no assumed base locomotion. Access tasks need a meaningful within-workspace, tool-mediated, or partner-assisted interpretation.

Mobile Manipulator

Base navigation plus manipulation, with validated terrain, clearance, reach, and payload limits. Mobility does not automatically permit climbing.

Humanoid

Potential whole-body, stepping, bimanual, or climbing actions only where supported by the specific body and controller. Height and reach remain measured variables.

Heterogeneous Multi-Robot

A team configuration containing explicit per-agent embodiments and complementary capabilities. It is not another single-robot morphology.

07

Three levels of capability knowledge

A / EXPLICIT · KNOWN

Use declared boundaries

Disclose calibrated specifications, for example reach 0.9 m, payload 3 kg, maximum step 0.2 m, and jaw opening 8 cm, with measurement conditions. Test whether planning respects these limits.

B / PARTIAL

Estimate from coarse information

Provide descriptions such as “mobile manipulator,” “small gripper,” and “low-payload arm,” without exact thresholds. Combine observations, prior knowledge, and interaction evidence.

C / LATENT · DISCOVERY

Discover the usable capability profile

Provide the body, a usable action contract, and normal sensor feedback. Withhold capability thresholds. Budgeted interaction reveals which movements and generic primitives are executable in the current context.

The numerical values are disclosure examples, not universal robot properties. Even Explicit mode does not reveal private world parameters or the correct strategy. Latent does not mean zero prior knowledge: body appearance and history are legitimate evidence. Audit URDF/config files, robot IDs, and debug responses for threshold leakage.

Cross each mode with Track A (generic skills) or Track B (continuous execution, including frozen VLA). Publish command syntax and observable execution status; avoid oracle precondition responses. An available command does not guarantee physical feasibility on every body. Keep the interface compatible within controlled pairs.

For disclosure comparisons, reuse the same validated physical instance under all three modes in isolated runs, with matched interaction budgets. Log capability probabilities, uncertainty, hypotheses, and strategy before and after probes. Exploration consumes the task budget; no free simulator reset or labeled feasibility query is available.

Freeze weights for the initial pilot. Within-episode belief and memory updates are allowed; persistent training or harness adaptation uses development splits and freezes before test. No memory carries between paired test episodes unless a separate adaptation setting explicitly permits it.

08

Private world state stays outside the agent process

Generator / evaluator / oracle only

Exact object mass, friction, global poses, simulator internals, feasibility labels, hidden solution graphs, and verifier source. Full TaskSpec belongs to this privileged plane.

Agent-facing observation allowlist

RGB or RGB-D; proprioception and joint state; mode-appropriate robot specifications; observable skill outcomes; optional calibrated force sensing. Perception estimates carry uncertainty and provenance.

“Skill failed” must describe observable execution evidence, not return a hidden explanation such as exact mass exceeding an undisclosed payload threshold. World coordinates estimated from perception are different from direct access to simulator ground truth.

Build an AgentView from an explicit allowlist; do not serialize TaskSpec and merely hide fields in the UI. Use separate processes/permissions for the evaluator, filter logs and error messages, and test for leakage through filenames, robot identities, reward channels, and API responses.

The hidden verifier grades final state after termination; no live feasibility oracle or hidden progress feedback is exposed in test. Public validity rules remain visible; exact physical limits follow the selected disclosure policy. Realizable observation channels support later transfer studies but do not establish sim-to-real success by themselves.

Reuse the open environment; enforce the discovery boundary

Open development / SWE comparison

Reproduce upstream source inspection, simulator debugging, and granted tools under each run's rules. Report this condition separately for program synthesis and infrastructure reproduction, without pooling it with restricted discovery scores.

Restricted discovery evaluation

Expose a public SDK, command syntax, and permitted sensor feedback. Agents may author solve(agent_view), but the object is an isolated RPC client with no raw env, controller instance, or simulator handles.

Retain environment and evaluator interfaces behind the boundary. Do not mount hidden configuration, verifiers, full URDF/asset metadata, or source revealing test parameters in the agent container. During testing, block raw-state reads/writes, reset/restore, physics edits, and replacement of the frozen executor. Enforce this with process isolation, file permissions, and an RPC allowlist, not prompts alone. Publish the generic SDK and development examples; audit logs, exceptions, paths, process access, and serialization for leakage.

The upstream solve(env) contract permits benchmark-source reading and blocks resets and direct state writes during fresh grading; development tools depend on run-specific rules. We narrow test information and permissions on top of that design rather than claiming upstream scoring is not hidden. Official evaluation contract.

Public model priors cannot be erased. Apply validated physical/control interventions without exposing their values, and report identity priors and memory effects. Isolate episode memory in the primary setting. Open development uses development splits: an agent cannot first inspect the current hidden test instance and then be evaluated as a Latent discovery agent on it.

09

TaskSpec is the source of truth

World ≠ Goal / Task ≠ Embodiment

Version WorldSpec (layout, objects, resources, and latent physical parameters), GoalSpec (objective and public constraints), and EmbodimentSpec (body and controller) separately, then bind them in TaskSpec. In Section 02's notation, T denotes goal/task semantics, W the world, and E the body/controller. The four reporting axes do not imply exhaustive combinations.

TaskSpec = bind(WorldSpec, GoalSpec, EmbodimentSpec, Disclosure, Budget) Same world + goal, different body → capability / strategy intervention Same world + body, different goal → goal composition test

One room can support high-object retrieval, delivery through a door, or clearing a passage, each with separately validated final states. Body comparisons hold the goal, initial world, resources, partner access, and budget fixed. EmbodiedSWE currently attaches goals to scenes while composing robots separately. Our extension concerns multi-goal semantics and controlled evaluation, not an architectural impossibility in that framework. Design notes.

Retain a simulator-independent task representation. Bind it to EmbodiedSWE / Isaac Lab first; later Genesis or hardware adapters consume the same supported semantics without making an engine-specific scene export the sole task definition.

Shared core: TaskSpec, RobotSpec, ObjectSpec, CapabilitySpec, ObservationSpec, Skill API, and success predicates. A versioned result contract and worker protocol connect execution to evaluation. Core modules contain no simulator imports; engine compilers map asset, physics, sensor, and controller intent without redefining the goal.

10

Share asset intent and validate the physical realization

Canonical robot asset: URDF

URDF → Genesis import URDF → Isaac importer → USD

Pin meshes, joint conventions, inertial data, limits, and controller configuration. Actuator behavior and sensor setup also require explicit backend configuration.

Complex objects and environments: prefer USD

Genesis documents USD loading, and the Isaac stack uses USD assets. Shared files do not guarantee identical contact behavior, joints, material interpretation, or visual output. Genesis USD · Isaac Lab assets.

TaskSpec carries mass, friction, restitution, joint limits, and dimensions with units and conventions. Backend adapters map these to engine settings and record overrides. Verify collision geometry, inertia, scale, solver/timestep choices, force/torque limits, and grasp behavior with calibration tests.

Capability fields describe measured envelopes. Reach is configuration-dependent; payload depends on pose, acceleration, grasp, and actuation. Derive capability changes from model/controller changes and validate them with task-relevant tests. Do not treat a scalar field as an engine-enforced physical limit.

For later cross-engine checks, compile the same canonical robot into both engines and cache derived assets by source hash, importer version, and options. Genesis accepts URDF; Isaac Sim provides URDF-to-USD import. Imported physics still requires validation. Genesis assets · Isaac URDF import.

Task generation: from interaction data to validated problems

Inspired by GRAIL's asset and terrain generation, sample geometry, layouts, objects, and bodies around task templates. Our proposed extension generates problems whose solutions must be discovered, with feasibility evidence and paired interventions.

Task template → sample scene / objects / terrain / body → bind TaskSpec in primary engine → validate physics and candidate routes → assign paired splits → release episode with private evidence

Keep constructive solutions private to the evaluator. Infeasible controls require justified bounds; controller failure is not proof. Generated videos or trajectories may support development, controller training, or private validation, but cannot become expert hints during testing. Record generator versions, asset provenance, seeds, and rejected candidates; audit split leakage through related assets and solution structures. The first pilot can use procedural geometry without the full video pipeline.

PRIMARY / ISAAC LAB

Validate the research question first

Start with supported robot/controller configurations and reproduce one task and grading flow. Validate and evaluate every primary-release instance on the pinned primary engine. Verify G1 stepping, tool use, and cooperation individually rather than inferring them from a body name.

LATER / GENESIS

Independent robustness checks

After the pilot, add a Genesis adapter for jointly supported rigid-body tasks. Stratify by family, constraint, and body; report coverage and disagreements. Do not assume throughput gains or silently discard unsupported fields.

Five canonical checks, staged by executor coverage

  1. Reach: direct retrieval versus an effective alternative.
  2. Payload: lifting versus cart/partner transport.
  3. Gap + bridge: placement, support, load, and traversal.
  4. Box + height: stable support and compatible stepping.
  5. Complementary robots: roles, access, and handoff.

Begin with reach and one resource-assisted route, then expand. Every released task requires primary-engine feasibility evidence; missing skills remain explicit coverage gaps. Grant cross-engine labels only after both engines' predicate checks pass.

Engineering revision: reuse-first development supersedes v2's day-one dual-backend rule and fixed Genesis-full / Isaac-10%–20% allocation. Retain simulator-independent TaskSpec and later Sim2Sim checks. The 250–400-instance scale remains a later candidate target, not a criterion for pilot validity.

Separate the agent, skill executor, and simulator

A contact plan is an optional intermediate representation. For example, hands ↔ crate; feet ↔ floor, then feet ↔ crate, then hand ↔ target. These specify intended relations. An adapter still needs executable targets or references, timing, support, transitions, and termination. Symbolic contacts do not automatically produce stable motion or reproduce SceneBot's complete native input format.

The agent must supply the task solution. A Track A adapter may expand a selected generic skill into local references. It must not consult a hidden solution graph to choose the box, order steps, or complete the long-horizon plan. Log the agent program, adapter expansion, and realized contacts separately. Return observable failure status; count recovery and internal retries toward the budget.

Integration gate: declare supported bodies, input modes, observation dependencies, skill coverage, transitions, and termination conditions. Validate skills and transitions before the full retrieval chain. Report native reproduction, cross-engine adaptation, and agent solving separately. ULTRA's IsaacGym / MuJoCo route requires adaptation to our primary engine; IsaacGym is not Isaac Sim. Compared agents share a frozen executor.

Sim2Sim harness: compare physical and strategic conclusions

Physics Agreement

Compare predeclared physical measurements and checks—force response, sliding, support, tipping, or bridge stability—under tolerances. Report disagreements and raw evidence, not trajectory equality.

Feasibility Agreement

Compare validated direct and augmented action/route predicates. Learned-policy failure is insufficient evidence of physical infeasibility. Use constructive controllers, targeted tests, and justified bounds.

Strategy Agreement

Compare which candidate strategies remain feasible and, when defined, their cost ordering. Different successful paths can agree semantically. Evaluate actual agent strategy stability separately from the validated solution sets.

C_sim2sim = matched resolved feasibility predicates / all predeclared predicates Also report: unresolved coverage, disagreement rate, and per-family results

With contact stochasticity, repeat trials, preregister decision margins, and retain an uncertain category. Two uncertain labels are not a match. Physics and strategy agreement use their own declared measurements; do not collapse all three scores into an unexplained average.

Tiered admission: the primary release requires validated core feasibility, intervention labels, observation isolation, and executor coverage in pinned Isaac Lab. A separate cross-engine validated subset requires predicate evidence in both Genesis and Isaac. Untested second-engine instances carry no cross-engine robustness claim.

Exclude cross-engine disagreements from the cross-engine validated subset. Unresolved primary-engine core labels also block admission to the primary release. Reassess after calibration and reruns; retain counts and per-family rejection rates. A disagreement may arise from import, controller, numerical settings, or a fragile task definition. Calibrate using physical evidence, not by tuning to preferred agent outcomes.

Keep the candidate population, the primary-engine release, and the cross-engine validation subset distinct. Report sampling and filtering bias. Agreement supports the tested predicates in two configured engines; both may share modeling errors, so it does not certify real-world transfer or universal simulator independence.

Five primary measures: from discovery to execution

  1. Task Success

    Hidden final-state success under public validity rules and a fixed total budget, including probes and partner work. Report success with sample counts and uncertainty intervals.

  2. Capability Calibration

    Before execution, predict P(feasible | action, context, evidence). Compare with validated private physics labels using Brier score, reliability curves, and ECE with fixed binning. AUROC measures discrimination, not calibration. Report false-feasible errors, abstention, and unresolved-label coverage. Separately evaluate predicted success of the deployed executor.

  3. Capability Discovery Efficiency

    Measure interactions, physical time, and cost until a predeclared estimation tolerance or validated strategy criterion is reached. For a fixed reach-calibration context, r = 0.95 m and r̂ = 0.97 m gives |r̂ − r| = 0.02 m. Report uncertainty intervals and budget-censored runs; never omit agents that fail to reach the criterion. For non-scalar capabilities, use held-out probe prediction error.

  4. Strategy Adaptation Score

    Evaluate trace-validated strategy feasibility across matched embodiments: required switch, efficiency-preferred choice under a declared cost, and appropriate invariance. Different feasible sets may still overlap. A strategy change alone earns no credit, and no single verbal gold plan is imposed.

  5. Constraint Attribution

    Predict reach, payload, friction, gripper geometry, mobility, collision, stability, or combined causes after failure. Use validated single-factor interventions and separately tagged multi-cause/ambiguous cases. Report per-cause precision/recall and probability quality where labels are identifiable; credit calibrated uncertainty where evidence cannot distinguish causes.

Oracle quality: labels require constructive solutions, targeted physical tests, or justified infeasibility bounds. Repeat contact-sensitive tests; record stochastic and uncertain outcomes. Failure of a learned policy is not a negative feasibility oracle. Scoring predictions happens in the evaluator and provides no online labels to the agent.

Supporting diagnostics: augmentation gain against resource/partner ablations, recovery success and cost, held-out embodiment generalization, and world-parameter generalization. Keep body-only, world-only, and joint shifts separate. Report category × constraint × body/team × knowledge coverage, and keep Composite results separate.

Matched baselines and ablations

Compare an embodiment-blind agent, specification-conditioned planning, reflection with capacity-matched memory, capability-aware planning, and capability-guided probing. Ablate self-model updates, world-model updates, attribution, probing, and resource/partner access. Give each baseline the same mode-permitted information and budget; oracle capability is a separate diagnostic upper bound.

Freeze before test and isolate episode memory. Use repeated paired seeds, per-family aggregates, sample counts, and uncertainty intervals. Log tokens, calls, actions, physical time, resets, partner work, and interventions. If claiming an optimal strategy, declare the cost function and evidence for optimality; otherwise use best-known feasible routes.

Solving with a fixed executor: add a diagnostic run with an evaluator-supplied known-feasible program to estimate execution bottlenecks; normal agents never receive that program. Report recovery rate, additional actions, and cost separately for natural failures and declared perturbations. Distinguish planning errors, contact/skill transition failures, and low-level control failures. Contact traces support diagnosis without imposing a unique gold contact graph.

Compare transfer assistance and capability discovery separately

The primary discovery condition supplies no successful source program. Compare the same coding agent with no capability updates, ordinary reflection, capability modeling, and active probing. Add a separate source-solution-hint diagnostic: source strategies remain feasible, become infeasible, or change only in efficiency on the target body. Score correct acceptance, rejection, or reconstruction. All matched methods receive the same public hint without target capability labels.

Report open SWE and restricted discovery conditions separately. Match observations, executors, actions, and budgets before attributing gains to methods. Evaluate generic-skill Track A separately from continuous-action Track B; supplied skill competence is not capability discovery. Add invalid-step identification, repeated futile attempts, and recovery costs as diagnostics, retaining the five primary metrics.

The comparison centers on embodiment-conditioned decisions

Closest comparison: EmbodiedSWE. Coding-agent long-horizon solving, cross-task/body transfer, solution diversification, and generated-task agent RL already overlap. This proposal must demonstrate additional evaluation value.

The project reports 28 tasks / 6 suites / 17 embodiments / 5 controllers. Its transfer experiments supply verified source solutions and measure target-solving benefits, including Franka solutions transferred to other arms. Official project and transfer experiments.

Shared ground already covered

Natural-language tasks, coding and simulator debugging, hidden offline scoring, pluggable bodies/controllers, transfer, and data generation. Increasing task counts, changing simulators, or adding G1 alone is insufficient differentiation.

Proposed additional test

Unknown intervened limits → budgeted probing → calibration and attribution → resource composition → strategy reconstruction. Test this chain with paired world-and-goal-controlled episodes, matched executors, and matched information.

Limit the claim: open source does not imply known capability boundaries, and transfer may include replanning. The reviewed materials do not establish the exact disclosure, calibration, and required-switch protocol proposed here. That is a comparison to investigate, not evidence that EmbodiedSWE agents cannot discover capabilities. Its README explicitly motivates characterizing differences between bodies. Framework design.

RLE-BENCH / ROBOTICS ENGINEERING

RLE-Bench: coding agents as robot learning engineers

RLE-Bench evaluates coding agents across nine task families in four categories: interactive control, policy development, perception and estimation, and mechanical design. Tasks provide engineering specifications, containerized workspaces, tools, and resource budgets; solutions are evaluated in simulation with visual and textual feedback. Official repository (checked 2026-10-10).

Relevance to this proposal: robotics problem solving, feedback-driven iteration, policy training, and embodiment design are relevant comparison points. Our proposed emphasis is controlled capability disclosure, self/world attribution, and strategy adaptation in paired episodes with fixed world and goal. Compare task protocols, information access, and budgets before claiming a distinct contribution; the repository overview alone does not establish whether RLE-Bench tests the same capability-discovery protocol.

Three complementary foundations for execution and generation

The roles below are our interpretation of official materials checked on 2026-10-05. Local integration remains unimplemented.

ULTRA / CONTROLLER

Candidate whole-body executor

Releases G1 control and training code, student modes full_track, sparse_track, and object_obs, and IsaacGym / MuJoCo paths. We would validate its supported skills; repository availability does not establish the complete move–step–retrieve chain. Official repository.

SCENEBOT / CONTACT INTERFACE

Reference for contact conditioning

Conditions control on reference motion and per-link contact labels, reconstructing scene-interaction graphs from motion. The project shows long-horizon interactions and a browser demo; Code / Data remain Coming Soon. Backend integration awaits release and validation. Official project.

GRAIL / DATA GENERATION

Scene and interaction data

Combines 3D assets and video priors, reconstructs 4D HOI, and retargets to G1; reports over 20,000 sequences. Our proposed use of asset generation adds task goals, private verification, and counterfactual pairs. Official project.

Reuse boundary: GRAIL's repository states that its code and derivatives are restricted to noncommercial use, except for NVIDIA and affiliates; third-party terms also apply. We initially borrow architectural ideas. Any code, weights, or data integration needs recorded component-specific permissions rather than an assumed permissive license. Repository license notice.

The evaluation layer: goal → capability / affordance discovery → planning → tool / contact use → execution → recovery. These works strengthen control and data foundations. Our benchmark tests solution discovery without task-specific references and strategy adaptation under controlled body changes. Existing long-horizon control demonstrations require explicit comparison; they should not be dismissed as lacking long-horizon tasks.

RoboTool

Creative tool use and reasoning about environment/embodiment constraints already overlap with this agenda. The proposed addition is controlled capability interventions, calibrated predictions, and measured augmentation. Paper.

RoboWits

Reasoning and strategy adaptation under unexpected physical conditions. Compare whether body-only changes cause appropriate strategy revisions, including controls where no revision is needed. Project.

RoboDojo

Comprehensive evaluation of generalist manipulation policies. Existing execution categories inform task coverage; our proposed protocol focuses on explicit capability judgments and interventions. Repository.

RoboColiseum / Genie Sim

VLA manipulation evaluation and simulation infrastructure provide relevant execution baselines. The comparison must align embodiment, observations, control, and adaptation budgets. Repository.

Terminal-Bench

Goal-directed problem solving with a declared computer interface inspires the evaluation structure. Physical capability depends additionally on the body, contacts, and controllability. Official introduction.

Other close comparisons

KinDER, EBench, PARTNR, tool counterfactual reasoning, and VLMgineer inform physical reasoning, capability diagnosis, cooperation, and resource design. See v1 positioning and the V3 reading list [external workspace].

Proposed distinction: evaluate capability discovery, self/world constraint attribution, and strategy adaptation under controlled body and world interventions, then test whether these improve augmentation and task success. This requires method- and protocol-level comparisons; it is not a claim that earlier work ignores capability limits.

First test discovery, attribution, and adaptation together

Smallest decisive setting: pin one room, a high target, support boxes, tools, receiving surfaces, and accessible partners. Build single-factor reach or mobility pairs. Ask whether evidence reveals why one step in an old plan became infeasible and supports a revised plan for the unchanged goal. Fixed arms, bodies without grasping, and bodies without stepping do not automatically have alternatives: validate tool, transport, and cooperation conditions, retaining infeasible controls where appropriate.

Primary hypothesis: under Partial / Latent disclosure, budgeted interaction improves self/world estimates and effective replanning and success over matched reflection baselines. Low success or apparent complexity alone does not validate the benchmark.
  1. Reproduce and isolate.Reproduce one upstream task and grading run, then implement a restricted AgentView with validated low-level execution and information boundaries.
  2. Establish paired evidence.Validate direct and resource-assisted routes; include required-switch, efficiency-only, invariant, and infeasible controls. Keep world-only interventions separate.
  3. Match disclosure conditions.Reuse body/controller configurations, randomization distributions, and budgets. The main condition has no source solution; hints form a separate condition.
  4. Log decisions before and after evidence.Capture capability probabilities, attribution, resources, and plans; relate updates to probes and realized trajectories, locating the step that motivated revision.

One physical instance fixes the goal, scene, body/team, and latent capability/world parameters. Generate controlled body-only pairs first; generate scene-only resource or constraint changes as a separate intervention family. Establish discovery and adaptation before scaling tasks and engines

  1. Reproduce upstreamPin commit, environment, assets, and robot/controller; reproduce one task and fresh grading.

Initial effort: prioritize reproduction, information boundaries, intervention labels, and matched comparisons. Retire the fixed 40% shared / 40% Genesis / 20% Isaac allocation and estimate work from reproduction and pilot evidence.
