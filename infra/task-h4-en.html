01 · Task description

What the agent sees, and what the scene hides

Parts marked "not visible" are for designers and reviewers only and must never appear in the agent's instruction.

Instruction given to the agent (verbatim)

A box rests on a pedestal on the far side of a gap in the floor. Bring the box back to this side and leave it at rest inside the green square marked on the floor, and keep it there for at least 2 seconds. You may interact with any object in the room.

The episode ends when you call finish(), when the simulated time budget runs out, or when the robot falls (pelvis below 0.35 m or tilted more than 50 degrees from upright). Falling into the gap is a fall. The box must not end up in the gap.

Use the robot-mounted observations and the supplied joint command interface. Your own code, perception, planning, IK/FK and the provided robot skills may help; they must execute through that same physical interface. You may not change world or robot state, attach objects to the robot, alter physical properties, read private scenario or scoring files, or inject observations or contacts. A private robot model for planning does not change the simulated world.

Call info() for the enabled sensors, actuator limits and episode budgets; observe() reads sensors. Send joint targets with command() or command_sequence(), and call finish() when done. Development resets are budgeted; evaluation and grading allow no resets. Grading runs your delivered program in fresh episodes whose layout differs from the development episode. Credit is given for physical progress along the task, judged from what happened in the simulator; the interface does not reveal progress or the score while you run.

Robot and interface (visible)

  • Unitree G1 with two Dex3-1 hands, 43 joints (MuJoCo Menagerie model, BSD-3-Clause). Torque limits are the real robot's: legs 88-139 N m, waist 88/50/50, shoulders, elbows and wrist roll 25, wrist pitch and yaw 5, Dex3 fingers 1.4-2.45 N m; the leg motors follow a DC torque-speed envelope. Silicone pads on the hands (friction 2.0).
  • Command channel as Unitree LowCmd: mode 0 motor (feed-forward plus PD every physics step) or mode 1 servo (implicit PD), clipped to joint, velocity, gain and torque limits.
  • Sensors (mounts from the G1 URDF): head D435 RGB-D, head MID-360 LiDAR, pelvis and torso IMUs, joint proprioception with applied effort, Dex3 link contact forces, foot forces.
  • Tools: L1 GR00T WBC (stand, walk), L2 MotionSolver.solve, L3 solve_waypoints, execution helpers (hold, move_joints, track). No task-specific skills.
  • Simulation: Genesis 1.4.3, 200 Hz physics, 50 Hz control, self-collision on, rigid bodies only.

Scene and randomisation (not visible)

The gap spans x 0.9-2.0 m, 1.10 m wide and 0.82 m deep; the goal is a 0.65 m green square on the near side at (-2.1, 0). The robot starts at x -0.70..-0.40, y -0.10..0.25, yaw +-0.25 rad. Every variable is drawn from (seed, episode index) before the layer decides which optional objects exist, so the necessary tools are identical across layers for one seed. A start check rejects interpenetrating layouts (0 rejections in 900 samples). Development seeds are small integers; evaluation and grading seeds are drawn privately on the trusted side.

Difficulty layers (the layer is not disclosed)

  • Layer 1: necessary tools only.
  • Layer 2: plus a 45 kg heavy board. It is long enough to bridge the gap, but on friction 0.9 it needs about 400 N to slide; a G1 pushes 60-100 N.
  • Layer 3: plus distractors. The 0.85 m short plank cannot span the 1.10 m gap, which depth or LiDAR can measure before committing; the crate on the far platform is irrelevant.

To be discovered (not visible)

  • The gap cannot be stepped across; falling in is a fall.
  • The propped plank can be pushed over its base edge past the vertical; it then falls by itself and lies flat on both platform edges as a bridge, and the four cleats drop into the gap and stop it sliding.
  • The push has a force window: about 19 N holds the plank at 44 degrees, and above about 30 N its base end slides away.
  • The bridge has to carry two crossings, the second with the box hanging from one hand while stepping onto the 4 cm plank end.
  • Capability classes: tool composition (plank plus props), structural mechanics, whole-body control, non-intuitive solution (the bridge is made by toppling, not carrying).

Version history

  • v2 (approved by the author, 2026-10-19..20): the plank propped on two props stood on end, plank friction 1.2, four 5 cm cleats, hand pads, 240 s budget.
  • v2.1: goal height range -0.05..0.32 m instead of 0..0.32 m (with the lower bound on the floor, a box resting on the floor had a margin of exactly 0). A defect fix.
  • v4 (the author's choice, 2026-10-20): the target is a box with a handle, carried hanging from one hand. The earlier two-hand box and the H1 folding stool could not be brought back reliably.
  • v4.1: the plank 4 cm thick instead of 6 cm. At 6 cm the G1 with the box fell or ran off the plank end in 11 of 20 step-ups; at 4 cm in none of 4.
  • v4.2: fixes the layer-3 short plank and heavy board starting 6 cm inside each other (found by the energy monitor).
  • 2026-10-21: the author confirmed the v4.1 plank thickness, the one-hand hold rule, the v4.2 fix, and the monitor rules and thresholds (h4-monitors/2).

The score is the peak progress. success requires delivery at the end of the episode, the route to the far side and back observed, and no violation from the task's own evaluator (task_spec.Evaluator, fed the same log); disagreements are flagged. Scoring reads only the trusted environment-side log; the agent sees neither progress nor score while it runs. Stage thresholds are initial settings, not yet calibrated on agent runs.

Alarms and handling (h4-monitors/2, frozen thresholds)

HandlingTriggers
VoidThe whole run scores 0: interface violations, changed world parameters, a teleport (over 20 m/s), privileged writes or gain writes above the command bounds in a recording, a broken request-log hash chain; severe physical anomalies: an object over 15 m/s, penetration over 10 cm, over 60 J of object energy not supplied by robot contact
WindowStage credit gained in the window, and any stage level first reached in it, never counts; the episode is flagged: an object over 9 m/s, penetration over 3 cm for 0.1 s, over 6 J without work within 0.2 s, arm shaking, finger jitter, sustained leg or waist torque saturation, pushing against joint limits, progress spikes
FlagFlag only: stages out of order, an infeasibility declaration, stage predicates disagreeing with the task's evaluator
Score 0A positive score without any observe() call is discarded; an unrun episode, a missing or damaged record, a verifier error, or a score that is not finite within [0, 1] scores 0

Thresholds are set so that no recorded legitimate run triggers them. Legitimate maxima: object speed 5.5 m/s (threshold 9), energy without work 2.5 J (threshold 6), arm shaking 2.47 (threshold 3.0).

Human check list (video review at admission)

  1. No reward hacking: every stage credit matches a physical event in the video: the plank toppled by the robot, the robot on the far side, the box hanging from the hand, the box carried back, the box let go and left. Partial bridge credit only after robot contact with the plank.
  2. Task really completed: at the end the whole box (0.30 x 0.18 m) rests inside the 0.65 m green square, and the robot went to the far side and came back over the bridge.
  3. No violation of physics or limits: no interpenetration of the plank and the platforms, no launched objects, joint torques within the real G1 limits (the trusted log records the applied effort of every joint at every control step).

04 · Reference expert

A privileged reference: topple the plank, then a one-hand round trip

solution/run_oracle.py reads simulator truth (object poses, contacts). It is a feasibility witness and the verifier's positive control, not an agent baseline. It runs under the agent's physics and the real G1 torque limits; the softened arm gains it uses for pick and place (kp 500, wrists 300) are inside the command channel's bounds; the recordings contain no privileged writes.

  1. Build the bridge. push_plank.py: walk up to the plank, soften the arms (shoulder and elbow kp 100), palms on the plank, and push it about its base edge toward the vertical by walking; the planned angle follows the body and never leads the measured one by more than 0.5 deg. Stop pushing at 86 deg and tuck the arms while side-stepping out of the lane at 0.3 m/s; the plank passes the vertical by itself, falls flat across the gap and its cleats drop into it.Median completion: plank engaged 19.7 s, bridge built 29.6 s
  2. Cross. run_oracle.cross: path following along the lane; after a run-up, step onto the plank end at 0.3 m/s and walk to the far platform.Median completion: crossed 46.9 s
  3. Take the box. handle_grasp.py: the contact QP takes the stance; the right hand makes a reverse hook (fingers down, palm out), slides index and middle finger under the bar and curls them round it, lifts 0.10 m, then brings the box to the robot's side by translating first and turning the wrist afterwards (a straight joint-space swing once flipped the box 147 deg round the bar).Median completion: target reached 63.1 s, target lifted 64.6 s
  4. Return. run_oracle.handle_return: back toward the plank until its end is about 0.5 m away, turn 180 deg clockwise on the spot, cross along the lane, pass the near props on their north side (between them the box hanging out would hit a prop) and stop with the box above the goal. While walking the gait policy sees the default upper-body pose.Median completion: returned holding 101.8 s
  5. Put it down. place_handle: the contact QP takes the legs, crouches to a 0.55 m pelvis with 0.2 rad lean, nudges the hand over the goal centre and lowers it 8 cm, waits 1.5 s for the swing to settle, straightens the fingers and lets the bar slide off; the box drops about 0.2 m.Median completion: delivered 121.0 s

Results

19 of 22 randomised episodes succeeded (layer 1 11/14, layer 2 4/4, layer 3 4/4), mean score 0.88, delivered at 116-126 s (budget 240 s). All 19 episodes that built the bridge finished; the 3 failures are all in the plank build: s109 fell as the plank fell; s112 the plank landed short of the far edge (its base end did not slide); s113 fell tucking the arms after the push. The verifier and the task's own evaluator agree on all 22, with no alarm.

The simulation is not bitwise reproducible on this GPU: the same scene diverges within the first seconds. The same 14 layer-1 scenes run once on v4.1 and once on v4.2 changed outcome 5 times, 2 of them because of the plank-build guard change and 3 purely from non-reproducibility. A single seed's outcome is therefore a sample of a rate, and grading needs 3 runs per episode.

What is hard

  • The moment the plank passes the vertical carries most of the risk: with the arms out, any pause of the gait can pitch the torso into a run; tucking while side-stepping was the steadiest of a dozen endings tried (20 of 22 in that family).
  • The plank's base end slides 3-6 cm during the push and another 17-24 cm as it falls, so where the base starts decides whether it spans the gap.
  • Stepping onto the plank end with the box hanging at the side failed more than half the time at 6 cm, which is why v4.1 made the plank 4 cm thick.
