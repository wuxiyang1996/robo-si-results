Task infrastructure · Task acceptance

Before a task is admitted, prove it
solvable and its verifier trustworthy.

This page covers how a task is accepted: the reference expert solution, official trajectories and subgoal scenarios, manual feasibility verification, verifier validity, the human check of the task and the automatic check pipeline. Grading agents is fully automatic; human checks appear only on this page.

Remaining work for 12 tasks: selected full witnesses, low-level actions and trajectory mappings

Design-feasibility gate: evidence the high-level-to-low-level, low-level-to-trajectory and high-level-to-trajectory mappings, with at least one generated, continuously successful end-to-end witness in a declared configuration. The third mapping may compose the first two and independently check the goals; no third planner is required. Existing full witnesses may be reused; a changed controller needs its own evidence. General rolling planning, continuous skill envelopes and arbitrary random-start composition are not prerequisites. Repeatability, random robustness, human review and second-person reproduction are reported separately; existing scores and admission states are not automatically changed. Acceptance deliverables

  • Task: predeclared representative scenes, selection rationale, complete runs and retained failures, versions and hashes. No complete solution for every random instance is required.
  • Skill: entry/exit and contact contracts, tested configurations/applicability, and critical short-segment evidence. Untested ranges remain unset.
  • Instance: high-level plan, selected skills and constraints, key states, check list and four-state result. Transitions check pose, velocity, support, grasp, remaining budget and uncertainty.
  • Budget: declare screening wall time, candidate count and short-probe count/steps before screening. Stop with unknown on exhaustion; do not automatically start a full simulation. Original execution time limits remain separate.
  • Report representative full runs, skill probes and static screening separately. Formal admission still requires verifier positives/negatives, physics/interface integrity and applicable human review and independent reproduction; screening cannot replace them.

Use the instance record template and versioned acceptance policy. The existing 15 admission gates and their status enum are unchanged; these four instance evidence states are distinct from passed/partial/pending/failed gate states.

Retain historical successes, failures, videos and review states. This revision grants no retrospective signatures or automatic admission. Existing explicitly agreed finite representative cohorts remain in place; scope changes require a separate record.

01 · Reference expert solution

Proving solvability and validating the verifier

  • The existing expert solution is enough: it reads simulator ground truth (object poses, contact forces) and plans with IK/FK and similar methods; it starts from random initial states drawn by the generator and drives the robot only through the action interface, under the same physics and torque limits as the agent; no teleporting, no editing properties, no attaching objects to the hands. It need not use the agent's tools.
  • Purpose one: a scoped complete witness. Demonstrate completion for the recorded configurations and provide reference times and score anchors; this is not a proof for every random instance. Plans and screening records remain separate.
  • Job two: validate the verifier. The reference expert's runs are recorded by the sandbox's own recorder and scored by the same verifier: they must be judged successful, stage completion times must match the manual labels on the video, and no monitor may raise a false alarm. Together with no-ops scoring 0 and negative cases and exploit runs scoring 0 or void, this validates the verifier from both sides (see section 04), as in Terminal-Bench, where the oracle must reach its expected reward and a no-op must score 0.
  • Representative acceptance videos are reviewed by a person; a second person reproduces the selected representative solutions. Static screening requires no video (section 03).

Conditions for the reference expert (oracle)

Allowed

  • Reading simulator ground truth: object poses, contacts, properties.
  • Brute-forcing intermediate points (keyframes) with IK/FK and interpolating them into trajectories.
  • Tracking with torque control or WBC.
  • Choosing and adjusting keyframes by hand.
  • The existing expert solution is enough; it need not use the agent's tools.

Not allowed

  • Starting from a hand-placed state: start from an initial state drawn by the generator (or a subtask start state).
  • Changing physics settings or relaxing torque limits.
  • Teleporting, editing properties, attaching objects to the hands.
  • Skipping a segment by placing things directly.
  • Bypassing the action interface to write joint or object state directly.

What a reference proves

It proves

  • The recorded initial configuration can be completed under the same physics, torque limits and action interface; this does not establish every random instance.
  • The upper anchor of the process score and reference completion times.
  • That the verifier works: the reference is judged successful, no-ops and exploits score 0.

It does not prove

  • That it can be done from the agent's sensors alone.
  • Whether a step every agent fails is too hard or impossible from sensors alone.

A reference is therefore not an oracle in the Terminal-Bench sense, where the oracle uses the agent's interface and information and must score full marks.

02 · Official trajectories and subgoal scenarios

Retain existing representative trajectories; cut diagnostic subgoals when needed

Official trajectories

  • The official scripts in solution/ and verified/ are kept unchanged. They place things with methods such as set_pos at the start, call the simulator directly at 200 Hz substeps and read ground truth at every step, so they serve only as privileged reference trajectories: proof that single skills are physically possible, and start points for subgoals.
  • For recording they run as black boxes; the recorder hooks in from outside and records constructor arguments, constants the script changed, physics switches, any privileged writes after the first physics step (expected to be none), the masses and friction of all rigid bodies, a privileged state log in the sandbox's format, 10 Hz full-body snapshots and the script's own phase timeline.
  • The verifier applies the same predicates as for agents to mark when the official trajectory reaches each stage.

Splitting into subgoals

  • World parameters and a full-body snapshot from any moment of an official recording (later also of reference expert runs) are restored as the start of a subgoal scenario (verified on H1: the restore matches the recording exactly).
  • After the start, the agent has only sensors and joint commands, as in the full task; the references' ground-truth reads and placements are not available to it.
  • Subgoal scenarios are for training, diagnosis and analysis only, reported separately and never mixed into the main evaluation, which always starts from the task's initial state (a subgoal's start state reveals the route).
  • Limitation: entities added outside the task environment (such as H1's stool) cannot yet be rebuilt from a checkpoint and are not supported.

03 · Representative validation and human review

Validate representative solutions and skill boundaries, not a full solution for every draw

Covers R2. Admission is scoped. Without a corresponding complete witness, static plans or separate segment successes cannot establish complete-task success.

  1. Freeze the plan and representative cohort.Declare layers/variants, routes, critical skills, selection rationale, screening budgets and a finite full-run list. Address boundary/interior cases and risky contact changes without requiring the Cartesian product of layers, samples and subtasks to run in full.Output: layered plan and predeclared coverage table.
  2. Execute a small set of complete references.Run continuously from original starts through the action interface under the declared physics, torque limits and scoring. Claims across backends need separate evidence for each.Output: full-run logs, seeds, hashes, verdicts and acceptance videos.
  3. Validate skill conditions.Check key reachability, collision, load, support and entry/exit conditions; use short difficult-action probes when needed. Snapshot starts support local claims only; composition needs actual handover-state checks.Output: skill evidence, applicability and unresolved ranges.
  4. Record random screening.Apply predeclared checks and budgets, assign one of four states, and retain all draws, rejections, failures and timeouts. A full solution is not an input requirement for each instance.Output: screening ledger.
  5. Review representative evidence.Review declared full runs and critical contact/negative clips against the three questions in section 05 and the stage timeline. Headless runs may be separate physics evidence, never a signed video review; static screening needs no video.Output: signed reviews and stage comparisons.
  6. Validate scoring and shortcut claims.Use the same verifier for full and stage positives, no-ops, negative cases and exploits. Screening passes are not complete positives. Unsuccessful shortcut search establishes only its tested scope, not impossibility of every alternative.Output: positive/negative and shortcut evidence.
  7. Repeat and reproduce.Repeat preselected representative seeds at least three times and report spread. A second person reproduces the declared representative solutions, not every screened random sample.Output: repeat and independent reproduction records.
  8. Accept by evidence level.Report full runs, skill probes and screening separately, retaining all failure causes and costs. Unknown and rejected results never count as successes.Output: scoped acceptance report.

Choose representative counts and rationale before execution according to risk and scope; there is no universal minimum of five full seeds per layer. Existing agreed finite cohorts are not automatically cancelled. Never replace failed seeds after observing results; narrow claims or add targeted evidence when needed.

04 · Verifier validity

Positive runs must be judged right; negative runs must score 0

The verifier has to be validated before its scores mean anything. As in Terminal-Bench, runs known to succeed must get their expected score, and runs that do nothing or cheat must score 0. Positive runs come from the reference expert solution: the existing expert, which plans from ground truth; its main job is proving the problem solvable, and its other job is validating the verifier here (see section 01). The videos of these positive and negative runs are also checked by a person when the task is admitted (section 05 of scoring and verification).

  • Positive runs: the reference expert's and official trajectories' runs must be judged successful (or reach their expected stages), with stage timelines matching the manual labels and no monitor false alarms.
  • Negative runs: a no-op scores 0; each stage's negative cases score nothing; escape probes and exploit runs are VOID or score 0.
  • Only when both pass is the verifier used for evaluation; rerun both after any change to predicates, thresholds or weights.

05 · The human check of the task

At acceptance and on revisions, a person answers three questions on video

At admission and relevant revisions, review the predeclared representative full runs, critical skills and positive/negative videos. Static screening requires no video. Headless physics evidence is separate and cannot receive a video signature. For each selected video, three questions:

Human checkWhat to look forMatching automatic checksIf it fails
1 No reward hackingWhether the scores of the reference and the pilot came from real actions; whether negative cases and exploit tests were all scored 0 or voided; any credit in the pilot obtained by gamingInterface and world-integrity monitors, predicate-anomaly monitor, negative cases, rollout provenance checkFix predicates, add negative cases or monitors, and check again; the task is not admitted until fixed
2 The task was really completedWhether the reference expert really reached the goal and met the contract; whether the stage times the verifier reports match the video (manual labels)Stage predicates (contact evidence, object identity, dwell time, rest conditions) and the success testFix predicates or thresholds; if the reference cannot do it, the task is labelled unready
3 No obvious violation of physics or limitsAny penetration, launching, floating or energy from nowhere in the simulation; whether the reference exceeded joint or torque limits or broke the public constraintsPhysical-anomaly monitor, command-pattern monitor, teleport check, world-parameter hashesFix simulation settings, assets (collision proxies) or monitor thresholds; a reference run that breaks physics does not count as proof of solvability

Procedure

  1. Run. The reference expert, a no-op, negative cases, exploit tests and the difficulty pilot, all scored automatically by the verifier.Output: verdict.json and videos.
  2. A person watches. Each video gets an answer to the three questions, with reasons and timestamps, paying particular attention to alarm locations.Output: task check record.
  3. Fix the verifier. Wherever human and automatic judgements disagree, change the predicates, thresholds, negative cases or monitors, then rerun the validity checks of section 04.Output: revision record.
  4. Freeze. Once all three questions pass, the verifier and the automatic handling rules are frozen; from then on agents are graded automatically only.Output: a frozen verifier version.

Every task provides a human check list for its admission that spells out what the three questions mean for this task (when the goal counts as reached, the public constraints, which methods are violations) and how each stage is recognised on video (a task card field; see section 11 of the task design guide).

06 · Automatic check pipeline

Every change is checked automatically; a person looks only at what changed

The human check of a task happens only at admission and on revisions; every other change is checked automatically by the pipeline, and a person looks only at what changed.

  • Static checks: nothing trusted in the bundle; the machine-readable task spec is well formed (section 11 of the task design guide).
  • A no-op scores 0; affected preselected representative references reach their expected outcomes and match manual stage labels. Screening passes are not full-run positives.
  • Every stage's negative cases score nothing; exploit tests are voided; escape probes are all refused.
  • When physical regression is needed, repeat predeclared representative seeds three times within the reproduction tolerance (protocol section 06).
  • Grading mode executes N sealed episodes under the existing protocol; author screening does not require a complete reference solution for each episode.

Status rollout: 11 tasks, excluding H1

Keep finite complete witnesses at task level; validate skill entry/exit, distance, load, support and pose conditions; screen reachability, collision, support and transitions within instance budgets, using short difficult-segment probes when needed; generate local trajectories from observations during execution. Deliver the plan, selected skills and constraints, key states and check results; full trajectories are additional representative evidence.

Keep witnessed_full, screen_pass, unknown and rejected separate. Missing checkers or exhausted budgets are unknown; rejection needs a necessary-condition counterexample and reason. Self-generated scenes may use validated skill envelopes; retain all prescribed benchmark samples and outcomes. Human sign-off, second-person reproduction and verifier checks remain separate, without reproducing every random instance.
