CONTRIBUTE

From task design to auditable delivery

Contribution workflow

  1. Design the task. Write the task card and machine-readable task spec against the eleven hard requirements of the task design guide.Output: task card. Nothing is implemented at this step.
  2. Design review. A reviewer checks the eleven requirements one by one, especially hints in the instruction, why the direct route fails, whether the solution is non-intuitive enough and whether the corner-case list is complete.Output: approval or requested changes.
  3. Build the environment and scene generator. Genesis (ideally with Isaac Lab parity), official models and assets, initial-state randomisation, three difficulty layers and subtask start states, as described in the task design guide.Output: environment, asset scripts, scene generator. Scale scenes with same-seed controls under the established three layers; report extra scaling axes and deferred layers separately.
  4. Define stage scoring. Specify subgoal dependencies, measured conditions, thresholds, dwell times, weights and final-success gates before evaluation.Output: stage rubric, trusted scorer and positive/negative checks.
  5. Integrate with the sandbox and test against bypasses. Escape probes, negative cases, exploit tests; a no-op must score 0; see the sandbox and scoring and verification.Output: test records.
  6. Verify the eight core deliverables. Retain at least one complete expert witness in the declared configuration, submit high/low-level planning and three-way trajectory mapping, and check stage scores. Validate physics and sandbox separately; record applicable human review and open items.Output: trajectory, plans/mapping, stage scores, physics report and sandbox report. No complete solution is required for every random instance. Also submit three-layer scaling and applicable embodiment capability-boundary designs with available evidence; label untested conditions explicitly.
  7. Difficulty pilot. Standard agent configurations try each layer; if layer 3 is solved in under 4 hours, add dependency depth, widen the random ranges or use distractors that are harder to tell apart (evaluation protocol).Output: pilot log per layer. Report results and costs per layer; where applicable, hold the world and budget fixed and intervene on body capabilities individually, retaining failures and unknown outcomes.
  8. Freeze and admit. The reviewer checks everything on the submission checklist.Output: a frozen version with version number, anchors and splits.

Delivery checklist

Base materials still include the task card, initial scene/generator, embodiment/sensor/action specifications, version hashes and reproduction entrypoint. Agent instructions specify only goals and constraints; expert plans and trusted evidence are withheld from the evaluated agent.

DeliverableWhat to submitHow it is checked
One representative complete expert witnessFrozen scene, assets, physics, controller and scoring versions; generator, full action/state/contact logs, video, result and hashes.Execute continuously from reset to final success. A matching existing witness may be reused. One witness establishes only its declared configuration, not all random instances.
High / low-level planningHigh-level subgoals and dependencies; low-level skills/actions, parameters, key states, entry/exit conditions, contact/support constraints and failure conditions.Explain how goals expand into executable actions. Leave untested skill envelopes unknown; a written plan is not execution evidence.
Three-way plan–trajectory mappingSubgoal-to-action segments, time intervals/action indices, key states, stage conditions, measured outcomes and evidence files.Check high→low, low→trajectory and high→trajectory/goal achievement. The third check may compose the first two plus independent goal verification; no separate planner is required.
Stage scoringStages, prerequisite dependencies, measurable conditions, thresholds, dwell times, weights, one-time/live semantics, final success and physical violation rules.Freeze before evaluation. Recompute stage scores from trusted measurements and align them with the trajectory timeline. No-ops score zero; stage counterexamples earn no credit for that stage. Violations or exploits receive zero or VOID under frozen rules. Partial credit is not final success. Record stage credit/progress, final success and VOID separately; apply the declared violation policy.
Physics validationContinuous physics records, contact/support/load checks, joint/torque constraints, interface audit, independent action replay and failed attempts.Prohibit state writes bypassing the action API, runtime teleportation, forced attachments, arbitrary external forces and ideal-state restoration. Document predefined physical structures and initial conditions separately. Initialized static tests and local probes do not replace a full trajectory. Report replay outcomes separately.
Sandbox validationAgent/trusted-side separation, allowed observations/actions, entrypoint, SDK, isolation and privilege probes, trusted logs and replay checks.Check hidden-state/scorer leakage and world writes. Report interface smoke tests separately from a full expert run inside the sandbox. Native physics success does not establish sandbox validation.
Scene difficulty scaling (three layers)Submit same-seed L1→L2→L3 scene diffs, tool/distractor lists, parameter ranges and per-layer results. L1 has necessary tools only; each L2 addition identifies an alternative body that can use it; L3 distractors specify failure causes and sensor-accessible clues.Keep the goal, required tools and their positions fixed; higher layers only add objects. Check obstruction, collisions, feasibility and new shortcuts. Retain representative evidence per claimed layer/variant and mark deferred layers unverified. Report dependency depth, tool combinations and random-range scaling separately; longer videos or repeated actions are not difficulty scaling. Retain necessity checks for L1 tools. For L2, identify evidence or pending checks for current-body inability and alternative-body usability. If tool combinations work, record another valid route.
Embodiment capability boundaries (where applicable)Specify implementable interventions on the body, end effector or validated controller capability, with matched controls. Fix the goal, world, resources and budget; vary one capability dimension through actual geometry, dynamics or controller changes. Record reach, grasp aperture, torque/payload and mobility/balance constraints with measurements. Revalidate physics and control after changes. Report test points and supported feasible/infeasible/unknown boundaries without extrapolating to untested ranges.Distinguish required route changes, efficiency preferences, an unchanged feasible route and no feasible route. A single failure does not establish a body limit; examine physical properties, perception and control errors. Untested interventions stay design/pending. Single/dual arms, fixed/free bases or robot families may be compared, but compound changes must be labeled and not attributed to one dimension.

The minimum feasibility witness is at least one continuous complete success in the declared scope; no full solution is required for every random instance. Repeatability, random robustness, applicable human review and second-person reproduction remain separate; retain existing preregistered finite acceptance cohorts.

Expert generation may use explicitly declared privileged information; a privileged reference is not automatically a public-sensor policy. Record physics validation, native action replay, sandbox smoke tests and full in-sandbox task execution separately.

We won't ask for

  • A sensor-only oracle that always succeeds.
  • Real-robot experiments.
  • Large-scale training; tasks are training-free by default.
  • Isaac Lab parity (recommended, not required).
  • A unique intended solution: valid unexpected solutions earn credit.

When claiming multiple difficulty layers or variants, retain representative evidence for each claimed scope. Mark untested layers and embodiment comparisons pending; do not infer full random-domain solvability.

Roles and review

RoleResponsible for
DesignerThe task card and machine-readable task spec; the environment and scene generator; the reference expert solution; manual feasibility verification; predicates, negative cases and the human check list. By default also maintains the task after admission
ReviewerDesign review; independent reproduction of the reference; the human check at task admission (watching videos and answering the three questions); checking the admission material
Task maintainerHandling flagged episodes; fixing the task and verifier, releasing new versions and triggering automatic re-grading; keeping the change log. Done before the next evaluation round
Evaluation operatorKeeping private material (reference solutions, verifiers, sealed seeds); running evaluations by the evaluation protocol, metering budgets, publishing results

Licensing and credit

  • Contributed code and documentation are released under the repository's agreed licence.
  • Assets are used under their own licences, with sources and licences in the task card; assets with unclear licences are not accepted, and assets that cannot be redistributed stay out of the repository and are downloaded by script.
  • The task card and task README name the designer and reviewers; papers or reports based on a task credit them accordingly.
